# Changelog for DungeonBlocks (Neoforge 1.21.1)

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [3.2.0] - Unreleased

### Added

- **Fan Cobbles** — a floor of small square setts laid in overlapping arcs, the fan paving of old town squares and courtyards, each arc three blocks across. Like the Flagstones, its joints run across block edges and each block picks its part of the pattern from where it is placed, so a floor laid by hand lines up into one sweep of arcs on its own. The arcs bulge the way you face when you lay the first block; every block laid beside it follows that block's direction, so the floor stays one way however you turn. Sneak while placing to start a new direction beside an old one. Cut from cobblestone in a stonecutter.
- **Mossy Fan Cobbles** — the same floor gone green: moss grown along the joints between the setts in drifts, so some stretches are thick with it and others barely touched, though every block carries some. The setts sit exactly where they do on plain Fan Cobbles and the two share a direction when laid side by side, so they mix freely in one floor without breaking the arcs. Made from Fan Cobbles and a vine or a moss block, or cut from mossy cobblestone in a stonecutter.
- **Sandstone Ashlar** — a floor of squared sandstone blocks laid in courses of mixed heights, each stone breaking joint with the ones beside it, for a desert temple, a tomb or a sunbaked courtyard. Like the Flagstones and Fan Cobbles, its joints run across block edges and each block picks its part of the pattern from where it is placed, so a floor laid by hand reads as one laid surface rather than a grid of blocks. The courses run across the way you face when you lay the first block; blocks laid beside it follow its direction, and sneaking starts a new one. Cut from sandstone in a stonecutter.
- **Drifted Sandstone Ashlar** — the same floor with the desert getting in: sand blown into the joints and piled where they meet, spilling a little way onto the stones, burying the joints in places and leaving them open in others, though every block carries some. The stones sit exactly where they do on plain Sandstone Ashlar and the two share a direction when laid side by side, so they mix freely in one floor, for a temple half-reclaimed by the dunes. Made from Sandstone Ashlar and a block of sand.
- **Wall Shelves** — a stone board on a stepped corbel, cut from large bricks in nine stones: stone, mud, deepslate and brick, each plain and mossy, and ossuary. Place one against a wall, and click its side to carry on the run: the board's halves alternate so a long shelf reads as long bricks breaking joint. It starts empty, for tomes and scrolls in a library or a mage's study. For an ossuary, use a bone on it to cycle through stacked femurs, two skulls, or a skull and femurs; the bone is not used up. Cut two at a time from a block of the stone in a stonecutter; the mossy ones are a plain shelf and a vine or a moss block.
- **Bone Chandelier** — four skulls looking outward with a candle on each crown, joined by femurs and hung on ribs from a chain, for a crypt or an ossuary's great hall. Hangs, lights and goes out as the iron Chandelier does, gives full light, and can hang from a Swinging Chain. A Chain over three Candles over three Bone Blocks.
- **Skull Candle** and **Black Skull Candle** — a skull with a candle melted onto its crown and wax run down over its brow, for an altar, a shrine or a catacomb's shelf. Place one on a floor and it looks toward you; place it on a wall and it sits on an iron bracket, looking out. Lit and put out like the Large Candles, giving a modest light. A bone block and a candle, plain or black.
- **Ossuary Bricks** — stone bricks bleached the colour of old bone, the base stone of a catacomb. Crafted from stone bricks and bone meal.
- **Mossy Ossuary Bricks** and **Cracked Ossuary Bricks** — Ossuary Bricks gone to ruin, for the older galleries. Made as vanilla's stone bricks are: mossy with a vine or a moss block, cracked in a furnace.
- **Ossuary Brick Stairs**, **Mossy Ossuary Brick Stairs** and **Hewn Tufa Stairs** — cut from their stones in a stonecutter.
- **Flagstones and Knucklebone Paving stack into walls** — Flagstones, Cracked Flagstones, Mossy Flagstones and Knucklebone Paving now keep their pattern lined up when stacked, as Grave Slab Pavement does. Blocks already placed line up once placed again.
- **Square Ossuary Brick** and **Left and Right Large Ossuary Brick** — one brick filling a block, and the two halves of a single brick spanning two blocks, for breaking up a long wall. Cut from Ossuary Bricks in a stonecutter.
- **Ossuary Bricks Slab Table** — the slab table in ossuary brick, one long brick along its top. Crafted from five Ossuary Bricks.
- **Hewn Tufa** — soft rock left rough by the pick, for tunnels and the plain stretches between burial galleries. Its four variants match at the edges, so a long wall shows no repeat. Cut from tuff in a stonecutter.
- **Mossy Hewn Tufa** — Hewn Tufa with moss grown over it, its pick marks left showing, for damp tunnels. Like the plain stone it has four variants that match at the edges. Hewn Tufa and a vine or a moss block.
- **Packed Skulls** and **Jumbled Femurs** — ossuary walls of bones set in the rock, with plain Hewn Tufa on top and bottom. Each block picks its part of the pattern from where it is placed, so a wall built by hand reads as one packed surface. Hewn Tufa and a bone block makes Packed Skulls; Hewn Tufa and three bones makes Jumbled Femurs.
- **Crumbling Bone Wall** — a false wall to hide a secret room behind. It passes for Jumbled Femurs at a glance, with a few hairline cracks for anyone who looks closely. Punch it, use it with an empty hand or give it a redstone signal and it shudders, its cracks opening and dust sifting out, then collapses a second later in a wave through every Crumbling Bone Wall touching it. The lowest blocks leave Bone Piles on the floor. Mobs walking into it do not set it off, and sneaking lets you mine it back without setting it off. Crafted from Jumbled Femurs and gravel.
- **Grave Slab Pavement** — a floor of worn grave slabs laid in rows running east to west, its joints crossing block edges so a floor reads as one laid surface. Stacked into a wall, its courses line up there too. Cut from Hewn Tufa in a stonecutter.
- **Knucklebone Paving** — a dark floor of pebbles with small bones set among them as a mosaic, also laid as one continuous pattern. Crafted from cobblestone and a bone.
- **Catacomb Niches can hold a soul lamp** — use a Soul Torch on a niche to set a blue flame in a stone dish of soul soil in its recess, in place of the remains. It gives only a little light, so a corridor lit by them stays gloomy and mobs can still spawn a few blocks away. An empty hand takes the torch back out, and breaking the niche returns it.
- **Cracked Bricks** — red bricks split by a settling wall, for ruined brick galleries; vanilla has cracked stone and deepslate bricks but none in red brick. Smelted from bricks.
- **Herringbone Bricks** — red bricks laid flat in a herringbone, for paved floors in brick galleries and courtyards. Any number of blocks join into one unbroken pattern. Stonecut from bricks.
- **Skeleton Remains** — a posed skeleton for set dressing that rests wherever you put it, not on the block grid: on a slab, a bed, a bench or half a block from a wall, turned to any of sixteen directions. Use it on a floor and it lies on its back with its feet toward you; use it on a wall and it sits slumped against it, legs out, head fallen forward; use it on a ceiling with three blocks of room beneath and it hangs there, shackled by the wrists to an iron eye. A bone cycles a resting skeleton through its poses: on its back, curled on its side, sprawled face down, and sitting slumped; punch it to pick it up. In the same bone white as the Skeleton block. Creative only, in the DungeonBlocks Entities tab.
- **Stone, Deepslate, Ossuary, Brick and Mud Brick Benches** — a stone bench you can sit on: a dressed seat slab on a support at each end, dressed like the large bricks of its stone, with a carved border round the top and a sunken panel on each end. Set benches side by side and they join into one long bench, the supports at its two ends and the carved border running round the whole length. A bench runs across the way you face when you place it; placed against the end of another, it carries that bench on. Use one with an empty hand to sit down, and sneak to stand up; a long bench seats one player per block. Cut from the stone's bricks in a stonecutter.
- **Mixed Ashlar** — a floor of squared grey stones of mixed sizes, a large one two courses tall among long and small ones, each dressed with a pale top edge and a shadowed bottom. The stones run across block edges, so a floor shows no grid. Cut from stone or stone bricks in a stonecutter.
- **Square Brick Stairs**, **Mossy Square Brick Stairs** and **Mossy Square Mud Brick Stairs** — stairs for the red and mud square bricks, as the stone and deepslate ones have. Cut from their bricks in a stonecutter.
- **Square Brick Facade**, **Square Brick Quarter Facade** and their **Mossy** versions — the red square brick's facades, as the stone, mud and deepslate square bricks have. Cut from Square Brick or Mossy Square Brick in a stonecutter (2 facades or 4 quarter facades).
- **Brick Rubble**, **Mossy Brick Rubble** and **Brick Rubble Scatter** — Rubble, Mossy Rubble and Rubble Scatter in red brick, for a fallen brick wall or a collapsed cellar. The scatter is laid and thickened as the Rubble Scatter is, and four come from a block of Brick Rubble. Mossy Brick Rubble is Brick Rubble and a vine or a moss block.
- **Ossuary Brick Slab** and **Ossuary Brick Wall**, their **Mossy** versions, **Hewn Tufa Slab** and **Hewn Tufa Wall** — the catacombs' bricks and rock now have the slabs and walls Ossuary Cobblestone has. Cut from their blocks in a stonecutter (2 slabs, or 1 wall).
- **Mossy Hewn Tufa Stairs** and **Square Ossuary Brick Stairs** — cut from their blocks in a stonecutter.
- **Square Brick, Square Mud Brick and Square Ossuary Brick Slabs**, plain and mossy — every square brick now has slabs, as the stone and deepslate ones did. Cut two at a time from the brick in a stonecutter.
- **Mossy Square Ossuary Brick** and its **Stairs** — Square Ossuary Brick with moss, made from Square Ossuary Brick and a vine or a moss block, or cut from Mossy Ossuary Bricks in a stonecutter.
- **Mossy Square Stone Brick** and **Mossy Square Mud Brick Facades and Quarter Facades** — every square brick now has its facades. Cut from the brick in a stonecutter (2 facades or 4 quarter facades).
- **Mossy Mixed Ashlar** — Mixed Ashlar with moss grown along its joints, so the stones' outlines still show. Mixed Ashlar and a vine or moss block.
- **Mossy Herringbone Bricks** — the herringbone floor overgrown with moss, its joints still showing the pattern. Crafted from Herringbone Bricks and a vine or moss block.
- **Soul Vent** — a dark iron floor grate over a pit of soul fire. It glows a cold blue through the bars and lets a thin haze of soul mist drift up into the room, with the odd flicker of soul flame and, now and then, a soul escaping with a wail. Put it out with a shovel or water, and relight it with flint and steel, a fire charge or a soul torch, just like a campfire. Crafted from a Dark Iron Grate and a Soul Campfire.
- **Giant Chain** and **Chain Anchor** — a chain of huge dark iron links, each a block long, hung straight and taut between two anchors up to 32 blocks apart at any angle. Use a Giant Chain on one anchor, then on another. Shears cut the chains on an anchor, and breaking either anchor takes its chains down and gives the Giant Chain back. Chains turn with a rotated or mirrored structure. The anchor is crafted from an iron block and coal; the chain from four chains and coal.
- **Medium Bone Chandelier** — the bone chandelier grown into a room's centrepiece, about two blocks across: eight skulls with candles on their crowns, on femur spokes round a vertebra column, a femur rim, ribs up to the chain. One block whose model spreads over its neighbours. Hangs from a ceiling, a chain or a swinging chain, lit with flint and steel. Crafted from a bone chandelier, five bone blocks and three candles.
- **Large Bone Chandelier** — a two-tier bone chandelier about four blocks across and two tall, for an ossuary's great hall: the medium bone chandelier as its upper tier, and a block below it a ring of sixteen skulls on a femur octagon, each with a candle on its crown, and ribs between the tiers. Hangs from the ceiling block above it; its lower tier hangs into the space below. Lit with flint and steel, gives full light. Crafted from two medium bone chandeliers, four bone blocks and three candles.
- **Medium Chandelier** — the iron chandelier at the medium bone chandelier's size, about two and a half blocks across: an octagonal wheel with eight candles at its corners and four more on a square inner ring, an iron drop under each corner. One block whose model spreads over its neighbours. Hangs from a ceiling, a chain or a swinging chain. Crafted from a chandelier, five iron ingots and three candles.
- **Large Chandelier** — a two-tier iron chandelier about four blocks across and two tall: an upper wheel of eight candles and, a block below it, a wider wheel of sixteen, braced to each other. Hangs from the ceiling block above it. Crafted from two medium chandeliers, four iron ingots and three candles.
- **Fire Basket** — an open dark iron basket of burning logs, hung on four rods, the fire showing through its bars. Hangs from a ceiling, a chain or a swinging chain. Burns as the brazier does: flint and steel or a torch lights it, a soul torch lights soul fire, an empty hand or water puts it out; embers can be placed by a structure. Sparks drop out of its bottom now and then. Crafted from a brazier and three chains.
- **Hanging Oil Lamp** — a terracotta oil lamp with a spout, hung from a dark iron bail on a chain, a small flame burning at the spout. Hangs from a ceiling, a chain or a swinging chain; lit with flint and steel or a torch, put out with an empty hand or water. Crafted from a flower pot, a chain and a string.
- **Bone Lattice Gate** — a see-through gate of bones woven in a diamond lattice inside a frame of femurs, a small skull on its top rail, for an ossuary chapel. It opens by hand or redstone and stands in line with iron bars, as the Iron Bars Door does, and villagers and zombies can't get through it. Crafted from five bones and a bone block.
- **Tomb Door** — a two-block slab of carved stone with a cross, standing on the wall's middle plane. Open it by hand or with redstone and it grinds sideways into the wall beside the doorway. Crafted from six smooth stone.
- **Oak, Spruce and Dark Oak Duckboards** — a plank walkway for mud and standing water, cross boards on two stringers, three pixels tall. They run the way you face when you lay them, and a run of them reads as one walkway. Six from three planks over two sticks.
- **Medium Chain** — a lighter chain of dark iron links, hung between Chain Anchors the same way, for rooms where a giant chain is too big. Giant and medium chains can share an anchor. Crafted from two chains and coal.
- **Stained Glass Window** and **Broken Stained Glass Window** — a thin panel of leaded red, blue and amber diamonds. Stack them in a column to build a tall church window: the top block becomes a pointed arch, the bottom one gets a sill, and a single block is a short arch-topped window on its own. The broken one has missing panes and grimy glass, for a ruined chapel, and mixes with intact panels in one window. Crafted from red and blue stained glass panes around an iron nugget; add flint to break one.
- **Ossuary Brick, Hewn Tufa, Brick, Mossy Brick, Cracked Brick and Large Brick Catacomb Niches** — the burial niche in the Catacombs' own stones. Crafted like the other niches, from the stone and a bone.
- **Left and Right Large Tufa Brick, plain and mossy**: the large brick pair in Hewn Tufa's stone, for a course of long bricks in a tufa wall. Stonecut from Hewn Tufa (the mossy ones from Mossy Hewn Tufa), or mossed with a vine or a moss block.
- **Stone Brick and Grave Slab Saw Traps**: a floor block with a circular saw hidden inside. When a player steps on one, there's a click, and half a second later the blade spins up out of the slot and cuts whatever is still standing there (2 hearts per cut). Mobs don't set it off by walking over it, but a redstone signal does, so a pressure plate, tripwire or sensor can arm it against mobs. The blade stays up while anything is on it or the signal is on, then sinks back and re-arms. The only tell is the slot across the top. The Grave Slab trap matches the pavement pattern around it. Crafted from the floor block, two iron ingots and redstone.
- **Cinerary Urn** — a bone-white funerary urn with a narrow foot, a wide shoulder ringed with an incised pattern, and a lidded top. Like the pots, it can be knocked over and shattered, and it throws bone-white shards. It also lets out a cloud of ash that drifts to the floor, and leaves what was buried with the dead: bone meal, and often a little gold or, rarely, an emerald.
- **Ossuary Cobblestone** and **Mossy Ossuary Cobblestone**, with stairs, slabs and walls — cobblestone in the Ossuary Bricks' bone colour, what they weather down to. Cobblestone and bone meal; mossy with a vine or moss block; the pieces are cut on a stonecutter.
- **Cracked Grave Slab Pavement** — the grave slab floor split and chipped, its cracks running across slabs and blocks. Smelt Grave Slab Pavement.
- **Chandeliers hang from Swinging Chains** — use a Chandelier on the bottom link to attach it. It swings with the chain with its flames following, lights and goes out as usual, and an empty hand on an unlit one takes it back off.

### Changed

- **Slab Tables redesigned** as refectory tables: a 4-pixel top with a leg panel and foot at each far end and a stretcher between them. Stone Brick and Mossy Stone Brick tables now show one long brick along the top, and table icons show the whole table. Tables already placed update on their own. The stretcher is decoration only: you can walk through it.
- **Square and Large Bricks can be crafted** — the stone, mud, deepslate and brick versions had no recipe, which also left their Capstones out of reach. Cut them from the matching bricks in a stonecutter. Their mossy versions are the plain block with a vine or a moss block, or cut from the mossy bricks where those exist. Their stairs, slabs, facades and quarter facades are cut from the brick: one stair, two slabs, two facades or four quarter facades.
- **More decorative pieces can be crafted** — every stone's arrow slit now has a stonecutter recipe, one per block, and stone, tuff and the mossy deepslates now have the rest of their pieces too. Mossy Bricks, Mossy Deepslate Bricks, Mossy Deepslate Tiles, Mossy Cobbled Deepslate and Mossy Tuff are crafted from the plain stone with a vine or a moss block.
- **Copper Offering Bowl's soul fire** is no longer drawn darker on one side: like vanilla's campfire flames, it is now unshaded, so it glows evenly from every angle.
- **Stonecutter fixes** — every stone now gives two facades per block; deepslate and blackstone stones gave one. Mossy stone brick pieces no longer come from plain stone bricks, and smooth sandstone and smooth red sandstone pieces are now cut from the smooth stone instead of the plain one.

### 🛠️ Fixed (NeoForge)

- **Brazier embers model** — it used Forge's `forge_data` key for its glowing coals, which NeoForge refuses, so the embers state failed to load. It now uses `neoforge_data`.

## [3.1.0] - 2026-10-30

### Added

- **Skull Pile** — the Bone Pile's grimmer twin: a heap of skulls with a few femurs wedged between them, for an ossuary floor or the corner of a catacomb. It builds up the same way, in four stages: placed as two skulls on the floor, then use a bone on it to heap it higher, up to six skulls nearly a block tall. Like the Bone Pile you wade through it rather than stand on top, and the skulls look the way you were facing when you placed it. Crafted from a bone block and three bones.
- **Catacomb Niches can be packed with skulls** — a new choice of remains that fills the recess floor to lintel, an ossuary wall in a single block. Cycle to it with an empty hand like the others, and freshly placed niches now sometimes pick it on their own, so a wall of niches mixes it in. Niches already placed keep what they hold.
- **Large Candles** — pillar candles of plain cream wax that stand on the floor, up to knee height, for lighting a crypt or a shrine. Place one and it starts small; use another Large Candle on it to make it medium, then large. Breaking it gives back each candle that went into it. Lit with flint and steel, a fire charge, a burning arrow or a torch in your hand, and put out with an empty hand or by water, exactly like vanilla candles. The bigger the candle, the more light it gives. Crafted from two vanilla candles.
- **Huge Candle** — a candle a block and a half tall and half a block across, placed and broken as one piece. Light either half and the whole candle burns, at full light.
- **Candle Cluster** — four candles of different heights burnt down unevenly in one block, for a group of candles without filling the floor. It is placed with its shortest candles toward you, so none hides behind a taller one. Crafted from three Large Candles. The candle textures are placeholder art.
- **Flagstones** — a floor of big irregular paving stones whose joints run across block edges, so a floor of it reads as one laid surface rather than a grid of blocks. The pattern repeats every three blocks each way, and each block picks its part of it from where it is placed, so a floor laid by hand lines up on its own. Cut from stone bricks in a stonecutter. Placeholder art for now.
- **Cracked Flagstones** and **Mossy Flagstones** — the same floor, worn: slabs split by cracks and corners chipped away, or moss grown along the joints in drifts. Every block of either shows its wear, so a dungeon floor weathered patchily, with plain and worn blocks mixed, still reads block by block. The stones sit exactly where they do on plain Flagstones, so the three mix freely in one floor without breaking the pattern. Cracked comes from smelting Flagstones; Mossy from Flagstones and a vine or a moss block. Placeholder art for now.
- **Deepslate Gargoyles** — the Perched Gargoyle, Gargoyle Bust and Gargoyle Statue carved in dark deepslate on polished deepslate plinths, so the watchers in a deep crypt match the stone around them. The perched one and the bust are cut from cobbled deepslate in a stonecutter; the statue is two chiseled deepslate on a polished deepslate.
- **Deepslate Altar** — a stone altar three blocks wide for a crypt chapel or a shrine: a plinth, a carved panel on its front and a slab top that overhangs all round, flat enough to stand candles on. Place it and all three parts go down at once, centred where you click and facing you; it needs the blocks either side to be clear. Break any part and the whole altar comes down as one item. Crafted from three polished deepslate slabs over polished deepslate either side of a chiseled deepslate. Placeholder model for now.
- **Blackstone Pedestal** — the Pedestal in polished blackstone, for a shrine in the dark deep levels: it shows one item on its top and can open a door when the right one is set there, exactly like the stone one. Crafted the same way, from polished blackstone.
- **Copper Offering Bowl** — a small old copper bowl, green with age, on a foot, for the top of an altar or the floor before it. Use it with an empty hand to change what it holds: empty, a pool of blood, or soul fire burning on soul soil, which gives off light and the odd blue flame. Soul fire won't burn under water, and pouring water in puts it out. Crafted from three copper ingots in a bowl's shape. Placeholder model for now.
- **Deepslate Ritual Circle** and **Blackstone Ritual Circle** — a circle chalked on one big slab of polished deepslate or polished blackstone, three blocks across: rings, eight runes, a ring of dots and a sun at its centre. Lay the centre first, then the eight around it: each block placed beside the circle continues it, so a circle can go anywhere. Placed by a structure it stays where the builder drew it and turns with the building. Crafted from polished deepslate or polished blackstone and bone meal. Placeholder art for now.
- **Wooden Bucket** — an oak tub bound with two dark iron hoops and full of standing water, a prop for a cell, a guardroom or a well. Like the pots it stands where you set it down and can be shoved about, but it never breaks: walk into it, hit it or drop it and it tips over, spilling its water with a splash, and lies there empty. Standing up it can also be empty: use it with an empty hand to tip the water out, fill it again from a water bucket, or scoop it out with an empty one. In creative an empty hand fills it as well. Hit one already on its side and you pick it up. Set it against a wall and it goes down on its side, already empty. Creative-only for now.

### Changed

- **Iron Bars Doors stand in line with the bars.** The Iron Bars Door, Dark Iron Bars Door and Tarnished Dark Iron Bars Door now sit in the middle of their block when shut, on the same line as the iron bars beside them, so a cell front of bars and a door reads as one wall, and the bars on both sides reach the shut door. Before, they hung at the edge of the block like a wooden door, a step out of line. Open, they still swing back against the side. Doors already placed in a world move to the middle of their block when you update; nothing else about them changes.

### 🛠️ Fixed

- **Manacles, Meat Hooks, Censers and Swinging Chains hang from a Wall Ring.** The ring has nothing solid on its underside, so they could not be placed beneath it, and one forced there fell off. They now hang from a ring on a wall or a ceiling; a ring standing on a floor still holds nothing.
- **Mobs now treat the three- and four-tall Dungeon Doors as doors.** The pathfinder saw them as a wall, so no mob that opens or breaks doors would ever route through one, and an orc hunting you stopped at it. They now read as doors, open or shut, wooden or not, exactly as the two-tall ones do.

## [3.0.1] - 2026-10-09

### ⚠️ Breaking changes

- **Corner pieces on Ledge, Facade, Fluted Facade, Quarter Facade, Cornice, and Crown Molding blocks change meaning.** Their corner shape is now defined *relative to the direction the block faces*, the way vanilla stairs do it, instead of by absolute compass direction. This is what makes east- and west-facing corners work at all — previously only north–south runs rendered and collided correctly. There is no upgrade path: **corner pieces already placed in existing worlds will visibly change orientation** and need to be re-placed. Straight (non-corner) pieces are unaffected.
- **The Brazier's `lit` and `soul` blockstate properties are replaced by a single `fire` property**, with the values `none`, `embers`, `soul` and `lit`. Braziers already placed in existing worlds go back to `none` (unlit) on load, and any datapack or structure that set `lit`/`soul` on a Brazier needs updating.
- **Requires GottschCore 2.7.0 or later** (was 2.6.0). 2.7.0 fixes blocks built on its waterlogged facing-half block coming out dry when placed into water; the Angle and Corner Plate Brackets relied on a local workaround for it, which is now removed.

### Added

- **Decorative entity props** — a new category of content. These are entities rather than blocks, so they sit anywhere in a block, react to being walked into, and are not limited to the block grid.
- **Terracotta Pots**, in three shapes. They fall under gravity, tip over when something walks into them, and shatter into a spray of ceramic debris on a fast enough collision or a fall of more than two blocks. Placing one against a wall spawns it already lying on its side. All three shapes share the name "Terracotta Pot" — they are a visual variation of one prop, not three things to tell apart by name.
- **Big Potion** and **Flask**, in red, yellow, blue and green — breakable glass potion props in two bottle shapes. They behave exactly like a pot (fall, tip over, shatter on a hard hit or a fall), stand about half the height of one, and throw glass shards instead of ceramic ones. Breaking one releases a lingering effect cloud rather than loot, with vanilla lingering-potion timing and a colour taken from whatever it carries. All four colours share one model and one set of dimensions per shape — colour is a reskin, not a different prop.
- A potion's effects are a per-instance property, set the same way a pot's loot is: an individual potion carries a vanilla `potion_contents` component under its `Effects` tag — the same form a 1.21 potion item uses, for example `{Effects:{potion_contents:{potion:"minecraft:poison"}}}` or `{Effects:{potion_contents:{custom_effects:[{id:"minecraft:slowness",duration:200}]}}}` — so a structure can make one potion a trap and leave another inert. **A potion with nothing set releases nothing**, and the bottle's colour is unrelated to what it does; a red potion is not implicitly healing.
- **Stone Pots**, **Red Pots**, and **Blue Pots** — the same three shapes in a cool grey stone palette, a brick red one, and a slate blue one. Each shatters into debris of its own colour: a pot's material is fixed by its type and travels to the shards it throws.
- Shattering a pot can drop loot. Each pot type has its own loot table (empty by default, and overridable by a datapack), and an individual pot can additionally carry a `LootTable` NBT tag — so a structure can put specific loot in a specific pot without affecting any other.

### ⚙️ Changed

- **Sills and Double Sills are remodelled with true slopes**, in every material. The sloped top used to be a block tilted at an angle, which stretched its texture and left a notch where the slope met the flat top; it is now one clean sloped face whose brick courses stay level and run on unbroken into the flat top. Both sit on the whole block's footprint with 12px walls and a gentle slope, rising 4px over 8: the Sill from its front wall up to its full-height back half, the Double Sill from both walls up to a low ridge along the top centre. Their hitbox and collision are now a full block, so they are as easy to stand on, target and build against as any cube.
- **Arrow Slit openings are twice as wide**, 4px instead of 2px, in every material. At 2px the slit read as a crack in the wall rather than an opening.
- The DungeonBlocks creative tab's icon is now the **Undead Pennant** instead of vanilla Mossy Stone Bricks, so the tab stands out among other mods' tabs.
- Ceramic Pot items render as 3D models in the inventory, in hand, on the ground and in item frames, the way block items do, instead of as flat sprites.

### 🛠️ Fixed

- **The stonecutter recipes for the decorative stone pieces are back.** Every Sill, Double Sill, Facade, Quarter Facade, Fluted block, Fluted Facade, Cornice, Crown Molding, Pillar and Pillar Base could be cut from its stone on Forge, but the NeoForge 2.3 releases shipped without those 370 recipes, and without the Mossy Chiseled Stone Bricks recipe. They are all included again.
- Chiseled Polished Blackstone can be cut into a Chiseled Polished Blackstone Facade. Its stonecutter recipe named a block that does not exist, so it never loaded.
- **Hay bales can be crafted from wheat again.** The recipe that turns Hay Patches back into a Hay Bale was saved under vanilla's own recipe name, which replaced vanilla's nine-wheat recipe for as long as the mod was installed.
- **Dirty Hay Patch has a new texture** — loose, trodden straw with the floor showing through, instead of a darkened hay bale top.
- **Doors no longer drop twice.** Breaking any of the mod's two-block doors — the Copper Doors in every stage, waxed or not, and the Spruce, Dark Oak, Crimson and Mangrove Dungeon Doors — dropped two doors, one for each half. Only the lower half drops now, as with vanilla doors.
- **Copper grates, heavy grates, valve wheels, trapdoors, heavy trapdoors and plate brackets drop themselves again.** All 64, in every oxidization stage and waxed, required the correct tool for drops but belonged to no tool tag, so nothing could mine them for a drop. They now need a stone pickaxe or better, like vanilla copper. Copper Doors and the Copper Sewer Block were never affected, but now also mine faster with a pickaxe.
- Dark Iron Grates and Dark Iron Heavy Trapdoors now mine faster with a pickaxe. They belonged to no tool tag, so a pickaxe broke them no quicker than a bare hand. They still drop to any tool, as before.
- **Blocks placed into water now keep the water.** Sills, Double Sills, Corbels, Facades, Quarter Facades, Fluted Facades, Cornices, Crown Moldings, Ledges, Barred Windows, Pillars, Pillar Bases, Heavy Grates, Plate Brackets (straight, corner and angle), Valve Wheels and Wall Rings can all hold water. But placing one into water pushed the water out and left a dry pocket, and it held water only if you poured a bucket on it afterwards. They now stay waterlogged when placed in water, as vanilla stairs and slabs do.
- **The Dungeon Lantern drops itself again.** Like a vanilla lantern it needs a pickaxe to drop, but it belonged to no tool tag, so nothing counted as the right tool and breaking one gave you nothing. Any pickaxe now works, as with a vanilla lantern.
- The Brazier, Candle Sconce, Torch Sconce and Wall Ring now mine faster with a pickaxe. They belonged to no tool tag, so a pickaxe broke them no quicker than a bare hand. They still drop to any tool, as before.
- **Arrow Slit blocks drop themselves again.** 30 of the 39 Arrow Slits belonged to no tool tag while still requiring the correct tool for drops, so they could never be mined for a drop by anything. Only the ones whose material name happened to contain a block-type keyword ("brick", "square") were tagged — meaning the Stone, Cobblestone, Granite, Diorite, Andesite, Blackstone, Sandstone, Tuff, Obsidian and most Deepslate variants were all affected.
- East- and west-facing corner pieces on Ledge, Facade, Fluted Facade, Quarter Facade, Cornice, and Crown Molding blocks now render at the correct rotation and have the correct collision box. Previously the two corner variants of an east- or west-facing run were drawn identically, and both fell back to a south-facing corner's collision box.
- Corner pieces inside a rotated structure keep their shape. A dungeon room placed at a 90 or 270 degree rotation used to come out with its corner pieces turned the wrong way.
- Corner pieces are now handled correctly in mirrored structure placements, which previously left the piece untouched.
- The collision box of a Crown Molding outer corner now matches its model; its lower moulding was two pixels shallower than it looked.
- Facade, Quarter Facade, Fluted Facade, Sill and Double Sill blocks (straight, inner corner and outer corner, in every material) no longer render almost black when placed against full blocks. These models reach the edges of their own block, so ambient occlusion treated the neighbouring full block as shadowing them. The models had asked for ambient occlusion to be turned off for some time, but the request was being silently discarded: Minecraft reads that setting from the *root* of a model's parent chain, and these models inherited from a vanilla model that leaves it switched on. They now inherit from a model that turns it off.
- Faces of Facade, Quarter Facade and Fluted Facade blocks that sit flush against a neighbouring full block are no longer drawn at all. Previously they were still rendered, taking their light level from inside the solid block next door and fighting with that block's own face for the same pixels, which showed as a black flickering seam.
- The tops of the candles on a Candle Sconce are no longer black. Every sconce except a north-facing one had its candle tops mapped onto an empty region of the candle texture, so they drew as solid black. North-facing sconces were the only ones unaffected.
- Two-candle and three-candle Candle Sconce models were also missing their transparency setting, so see-through parts of the candle texture were drawn as solid black. The one-candle model was already correct.
- The top face of each candle on a Candle Sconce now shows the wax top of the candle rather than a slice of the candle's side.
- The third arm of the Candle Sconce sat almost entirely outside its own block, roughly three pixels into the neighbouring block. It is now positioned as a true mirror of the first arm, so the three arms are evenly spaced.
- 265 block models across 20 base models no longer render one or more faces as a magenta/black missing-texture checkerboard. Affected: every Crown Molding piece, Corbel, Barred Window Facade, Ledge inner corners, five Dungeon Door pieces, three Heavy Trapdoor pieces, two Sewer pieces, Wall Ring, Torch Sconce, and all three Braziers. The models referenced a texture variable nothing in their parent chain ever declared.
- Pillar Base blocks now render facing the direction they were placed against (previously always rendered in the default orientation, though the collision box rotated correctly).
- The side of a Sill block no longer shows a seam down its middle. The sill is built from two halves and both were drawing the same half of the texture, so the pattern restarted midway along the block. The underside had the same fault. Cornice, Facade and Quarter Facade inner corners were affected in the same way.
- Angle Cobwebs break quickly again. A sword now cuts one in 8 ticks, the same as a vanilla cobweb, and anything else takes 12. They previously took a full 20 seconds to break with any tool and then dropped nothing at all: they took their hardness and their "requires the correct tool" flag from the vanilla cobweb, but nothing counts as the correct tool for them, because vanilla only grants a sword its speed and its harvesting ability on the vanilla cobweb block specifically.
- A Crown Molding outer corner now blends with the straight pieces either side of it. Its top surface was taking its colours from the corner of the texture, where many materials keep a lighter edge, while its neighbours took theirs from the middle — so the corner read as a brighter patch rather than as a continuation of the run.
- Dungeon Lanterns and Sconces placed by a structure, a datapack or a command are no longer waterlogged. Their default state had every on/off property switched on, so anything that started from the default and did not explicitly say otherwise got a lantern that was hanging and full of water, and the water poured out across the floor around it. Lanterns and sconces placed by hand were never affected.

### Known limitations

- Swinging chains must hang from a ceiling or from another chain, and cannot be placed in mid-air.
- Ceramic Pots drop nothing by default; the loot plumbing is in place but the shipped loot tables are intentionally empty.
- Tall doors have no crafting recipe yet (obtainable via creative/give only).
- Square Stone Brick and Square Mud Brick stairs and facades have no crafting recipe yet (obtainable via creative/give only).
- Polished Andesite Brick blocks and stairs, in both plain and mossy, have no crafting recipe yet (obtainable via creative/give only), and neither do Mossy Square Stone Brick Stairs.
